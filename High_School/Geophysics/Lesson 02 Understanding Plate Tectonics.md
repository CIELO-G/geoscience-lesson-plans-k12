# Lesson Plan: Understanding Plate Tectonics

**Grade Level:** 4th–12th Grade  
**Subject:** Science  
**Duration:** 45 minutes

---

## OVERVIEW

This lesson introduces students to the fundamentals of plate tectonics through a hands-on Oreo activity. Students will investigate the three types of plate boundaries by modeling their movements and exploring how these interactions create Earth's major landforms and natural hazards, including mountains, ocean trenches, volcanoes, and earthquakes.

---

## OBJECTIVES

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- Identify and describe the three types of plate boundaries: convergent, divergent, and transform.
- Demonstrate how plate movements produce earthquakes, volcanoes, and mountain formation.
- Explain the importance of plate tectonics in shaping Earth's surface.
- Relate real-world examples of mountains, trenches, volcanoes, and earthquakes to each type of plate boundary.

---

## MATERIALS

- Plate Tectonics PowerPoint presentation
- Paper plates
- Frosting (to represent the asthenosphere/lithosphere)
- Oreo cookies (to represent tectonic plates)
- Student worksheets
- Whiteboard and markers

---

## PROCEDURE

### 1. Engage (5 minutes)

- Present a short PowerPoint introducing plate tectonics.
- Ask students the following questions:
  - What is a force, and how are forces applied to Earth?
  - What is a tectonic plate?
  - What forces act at different plate boundaries?

---

### 2. Explore (10 minutes)

- Have students spread frosting onto a paper plate to represent the Earth's upper mantle.
- Place Oreo cookies on top of the frosting to represent tectonic plates.
- Guide students in modeling:
  - Divergent boundaries (plates moving apart)
  - Convergent boundaries (plates moving together)
  - Transform boundaries (plates sliding past one another)
- Encourage students to observe and discuss the pushing, pulling, and sliding forces involved in each movement.

---

### 3. Explain (10 minutes)

- Draw cross-sectional diagrams of the three plate boundaries.
- Explain the direction of forces acting at each boundary.
- Discuss how each boundary produces different geologic features and natural hazards.
- Have students draw arrows indicating force directions on their worksheets.

---

### 4. Elaborate (10 minutes)

- Have students work in pairs to complete the worksheet.
- Ask students to identify real-world examples of each boundary type and explain the landforms or hazards associated with them.

---

### 5. Evaluate (10 minutes)

- Review worksheet responses through class discussion or a short oral quiz.
- Have students write a brief reflection describing:
  - What they learned about plate tectonics.
  - How plate movements shape Earth's surface.
  - Why understanding plate tectonics is important.

---

## ASSESSMENT

- Observation of student participation during the Oreo plate tectonics activity.
- Accuracy and completeness of student worksheets.
- Student responses during class discussion or oral questioning.
- Reflection paragraph demonstrating understanding of plate boundaries and their effects on Earth.
