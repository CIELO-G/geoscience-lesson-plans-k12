# Lesson Plan: Lesson 04 The Wave Machine Lab

**Grade Level:** 9th–12th Grade  
**Subject:** Science  
**Duration:** 1 hour

---

## OVERVIEW

This lab demonstrates how to build a wave machine that simulates a transverse wave. Students will observe wave properties such as amplitude, frequency, wavelength, speed, and velocity. Understanding wave properties and how waves move through different materials helps scientists study materials that cannot be directly seen.

Geophysicists use seismic waves to understand the material layers beneath Earth's surface. By analyzing seismic wave properties such as amplitude, frequency, wavelength, speed, and velocity, scientists can determine where earthquakes occur, identify underground resources and hazards, and investigate the structure of Earth.

In this lab, the seismic wave related to the model is an S-wave, or shear wave. An S-wave is a transverse wave that moves through the body of Earth. It travels through solids, and its velocity generally increases with depth and pressure. However, S-waves cannot travel through liquids because liquids do not support shearing motion. Students will first simulate a one-layer model representing the same material, then transition to a two-layer model representing two different materials.

---

## OBJECTIVES

By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:

- Identify and describe the characteristics of a wave.
- Demonstrate understanding of how waves move through materials.
- Connect wave physics properties to Earth processes.

---

## MATERIALS

- Duct tape
- Wooden skewers, approximately 30 cm or 12 inches in length
- Gummy bears, large bag
- Two anchoring items
- Timer, stopwatch, or phone
- Ruler or meter stick

---

## PROCEDURE

### 1. Engage (5 minutes)

- Draw a sine wave on the board.
- Ask students to identify the following wave features:
  - Wavelength
  - Amplitude
  - Trough
  - Crest
- Ask students:
  - How do we calculate the velocity of a wave?
  - What two values need to be identified?

---

### 2. Explore (35 minutes)

- Divide students into groups of 4–5.
- Have each group build a wave machine using duct tape, wooden skewers, and gummy bears.
- Use the wave machine to demonstrate how a transverse wave, or S-wave, moves through one material layer.
- Have students record observations for the one-layer model.
- Then have students remove some gummy bears from slightly more than half of the wave machine.
- Repeat the wave demonstration and observations.
- Explain that this second setup represents a two-layer or two-material system.

---

### 3. Explain (10 minutes)

- Have students fill out the tables in the lab report.
- Ask students to answer the lab questions using their observations.
- Discuss how changes in material properties affect wave motion, speed, and behavior.

---

### 4. Elaborate (15 minutes)

- Have each group select one student to share the group's observations.
- Ask students to compare the one-layer and two-layer models.
- Discuss how this relates to seismic waves moving through different layers of Earth.

---

### 5. Evaluate (10 minutes)

- Have students turn in their completed lab reports.
- Students may submit individual reports or one group report with all group members' names included.

---

## ASSESSMENT

- Observation of student participation and inquiry during the wave machine activity.
- Accuracy and clarity of group presentations.
- Completed lab report with clear answers and filled-out tables.
